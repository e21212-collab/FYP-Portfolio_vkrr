Research Background

About This Project

Cancellation of Self-Sensing Due to Ego-Motion of Vibration Based Mechanosensors

The Problem

Why Does Self-Sensing Matter?

A vibration-based artificial robotic antenna (feeler), inspired by insect antennae, is constructed using an accelerometer mounted on the tip of a plastic tube. This antenna is mounted on a Pan-Tilt unit, which is attached to a mobile robot. The antenna detects contacts through vibration signals.

The problem arises when the robot moves — this ego-motion generates vibrations that the accelerometer cannot distinguish from real contact events. This is called the self-sensing problem: the sensor "feels" the robot's own motion instead of only the external world.

The Approach

How We Tackle Ego-Motion Cancellation

This research investigates multiple strategies to cancel ego-motion-induced self-sensing:

  • Reference Sensor Subtraction: A dedicated IMU or accelerometer captures the ego-motion signal, which is then subtracted from the mechanosensor output.
  • Adaptive Filtering: Algorithms such as Least Mean Squares (LMS) dynamically update filter coefficients to cancel the correlated noise component.
  • Frequency Domain Separation: Where ego-motion and external signals occupy different frequency bands, bandpass filtering can isolate the signal of interest.
  • Machine Learning Classification: Trained models can distinguish ego-motion artefacts from genuine tactile events.
