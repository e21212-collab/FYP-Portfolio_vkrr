Week 1

Literature Review

Completed

Focus: Understanding the existing body of knowledge

Objective:Initiated the project by exploring the biological principles of active tactile sensing and identifying the mechanisms organisms use to manage self-stimulation during movement.

Progress: Analyzed how biological systems coordinate active antennal movements and process mechanoreceptive inputs to establish a conceptual baseline for ego-motion cancellation.

Literature Reviewed & Repository Links:

  • Staudacher et al.(2005) - Antennal Movements and Mechanoreception:Neurobiology of Active Tactile Sensors

    Evaluated the integration of exteroceptive and proprioceptive cues required to distinguish external environmental interactions from self-induced mechanical movement.

  • Harischandra et al.(2015) — PStable phase-shift despite quasi-rhythmic movements: a CPG-driven dynamic model of active tactile exploration in an insect

    Studied Central Pattern Generator (CPG) modeling frameworks and phase-coupled oscillators used to capture quasi-rhythmic tactile exploration trajectories.

  • Hoinville et al. — Insect-Inspired Tactile Contour Sampling Using Vibration-Based Robotic Antennae

    Studied Central Pattern Generator (CPG) modeling frameworks and phase-coupled oscillators used to capture quasi-rhythmic tactile exploration trajectories.

Literature Reviewed & Repository Links:

  • Harischandra & Dürr (2012) - A Forward Model for an Active Tactile Sensor Using Echo State Networks

    Examined the utilization of Echo State Networks (ESNs) as forward models to effectively separate self-motion acceleration signatures from genuine external contact events.

  • Harischandra & Dürr (2013) — Prediction of Self-induced Mechanoreceptive Sensor Readings in an Insect-inspired Active Tactile Sensing System

    Reviewed methods to build internal models capable of predicting mechanoreceptive feedback during active robot locomotion.

  • Gunasekara et al. (2019) — Distance Estimation and Material Classification of a Compliant Tactile Sensor Using Vibration Modes and Support Vector Machine

    Investigated how physical vibration patterns adapt based on contact parameters, leveraging frequency-domain analysis and machine learning (SVM/ANN) to classify tactile states.

    Platform Observations: 4WD Omni-Directional Mobile Base

    The selected platform uses a holonomic drive system with 90° omnidirectional wheels, allowing the robot to move in any direction; forwards, sideways, and rotational, without changing wheel orientation. This is significant for ego-motion testing as it enables precise and repeatable motion profiles in multiple directions. Key specifications noted: aluminium alloy frame (rigid, minimises unwanted flex), 0.6 m/s top speed, 10 kg load capacity (sufficient for all components), and 100 mm polyurethane omni wheels. Unlike a standard 2WD differential robot, the holonomic configuration gives full 3-DOF planar motion control, which will allow more varied ego-motion scenarios during data collection.

    • Week 7

    Coding & Logic Framework Development

    Completed

    Focus: Development of embedded firmware for real-time sensor acquisition, actuator control, and telemetry communication.

    Objective: Develop and validate the Arduino firmware required for the bio-inspired tactile sensing platform. The objective was to establish reliable communication with the MPU6050 IMU, acquire real-time vibration data, implement continuous servo-based antenna motion, and transmit the collected sensor data through a high-speed serial interface for future Echo State Network (ESN) training.

    Progress:

    Stage 1

    The first stage focused on establishing communication between the Arduino and the MPU6050 using the I²C protocol. The accelerometer was configured with an appropriate measurement range and digital filter settings, enabling continuous acquisition of three-axis acceleration data (Ax, Ay, and Az). The measurements were successfully streamed to the host computer through a 115200 baud serial communication interface, confirming reliable real-time telemetry.

    Stage 2

    After validating the IMU communication, the firmware was extended to include two servo motors that generate continuous sinusoidal motion, simulating the oscillatory movement of insect antennae. While the servos were moving, the MPU6050 continuously recorded the resulting vibration profile and streamed the measurements to the host computer. This established the first synchronized sensing framework required for future ESN-based prediction and tactile event detection.
